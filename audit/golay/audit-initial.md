**Verdict. NOT-BROKEN.** The two source pairs need not commute with one another. For instance the constant pairs (i,1) and (s,1) each qualify and i does not commute with s. Thus plain juxtaposition in one copy of the dihedral algebra would require a new argument. There is an independent remedy at the proposed order.

Represent each group element by its real signed permutation matrix of order two, with transpose implementing the involution. Put every first-pair coefficient in the first tensor factor of two such algebras and every second-pair coefficient in the second. Their Hall polynomials and adjoints now commute across pairs. Substituting the cyclic shift of order L=m+n preserves the commuting identities within each pair. Consequently the four circulants obtained by concatenating (a,c), (a,-c), (b,d), (b,-d), and their adjoints commute. Their Gram sums equal 4L times the identity by cancellation of the two cross terms in each sign doublet. The cyclic reversal tensored with the signed permutation s⊗s interchanges each such circulant with its transpose by conjugation, because sxs=x-transpose separately for x=±1,±i,±s. This gives the required reversal for a four-block Goethals–Seidel matrix. Each matrix entry at the group level is a signed permutation of degree four, including after reversal and adjoints. Right multiplying each such block by an ordinary H4 densifies the resulting array and scales its Gram matrix by four. This predicts order 16L. I have reserved the explicit block-array multiplication and executable checks for the proof-aware stage.
