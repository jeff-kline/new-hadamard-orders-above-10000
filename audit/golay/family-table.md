# All odd parts through 2,999

The order formula 16(a+b) lowers 34 of the 1,500 exponents in Cati and Pasechnik's Table 4. It lowers three of the 1,500 exponents in Kumar's November 24 snapshot. Two go from 5 to 4, at 1,567 and 2,887. The third goes from 6 to 5, at 1,499. Their respective length sums are 7+1560, 7+2880 and 182+2816. Taking two independently chosen dihedral pairs introduces no additional improvement over Kumar. An independent consequence of Du–Jiang lowers a fourth Kumar entry, 2,995, from 5 to 4. Its complementary quadruple has length 5(550+648)=5990. These are comparisons of published table entries. Earlier derivations and limitations on historical priority are documented in [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) and [mixed-priority.md](mixed-priority.md). [Cati and Pasechnik, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18897v2) · [Kumar's pinned frontier](https://github.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/blob/d885022144ba6bf4efa8315e14871a3736947527/FRONTIER.md) · [Du–Jiang, Corollaries 3 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381)

Run `node family-table.mjs` in this directory for all 34 witnesses and exponents. C denotes a scalar complex pair of a length certified by Du–Jiang (18). D indicates a dihedral pair of one of the three seed lengths multiplied by the displayed binary Golay length. Both factors range over their full union. The Cati exceptions file was extracted from Table 4 of the v2 PDF with pdftotext -layout. The Kumar file records the odd part of each of the 120 rows of its unresolved-orders table. The 28 higher exponents come from the three lists in its preceding text. All remaining rows have the default exponent 2 in the respective table conventions. The program checks the two source row counts. [Kamali and Kharaghani, pp. 139–144](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/18/ocr-ajc-v18-p139.pdf) · [Du–Jiang, equation (18)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381)

A strict improvement of either tabulation needs exponent at most 8. Thus every successful length sum is at most 2999·16. Removing all powers of two from a+b gives its complete odd-part accounting. Doubling can never lower a best exponent. A separate dynamic program tests every odd factorization recursively using Agaian's multiplication, taking exponents s,t to s+t−1. This also covers ordinary tensor multiplication, whose exponent is higher by one. For Cati's table the union of direct constructions and multiplication improves 37 entries, three already following from its own input matrices (1793, 2119 and 2315). Comparing multiplication closure on each side leaves exactly the original 34. Multiplication yields no additional Kumar improvements. All these comparisons include composite odd parts. [Kumar's explanation of multiplication and the three entries](https://github.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/blob/d885022144ba6bf4efa8315e14871a3736947527/FRONTIER.md#L33-L57)

## Conditional length-19 comparison

The arithmetic for the separate length-19 construction uses 16(19b+b′), 32(19b+c) and 32(19b+19b′), with binary Golay lengths b,b′ and a length c from either original alphabet. Eleven Cati rows improve under one of these formulas: 739, 1223, 1439, 1447, 1499, 1987, 2039, 2399, 2687, 2879 and 2999. The union with the original family therefore lowers 35 Cati entries. The variant further lowers 2879 from the original family's exponent 6 to 5, using 32(19+2860). It adds 2999 at exponent 6, using 32(190+5808), which agrees with Kumar's listed exponent. Neither these three formulas nor their union with the original family yields a fourth strict Kumar improvement. This comparison tests arithmetic consequences only.

## An independent comparison for 2,999

Craigen's 1995 Theorem 6(2) and the four complementary binary words of length 749 documented in [the classical audit](audit-classical.md) give an earlier matrix at 128·2999 = 383872. Take t=2 and L=749+749=1498. The theorem gives 2·4³(2L+3). In contrast, Theorem 9 gives exponent 4⌈log₆₄(1499)⌉+2=10. Thus the two documented earlier sources giving exponents 9 and 6 respectively have different evidentiary weight: Cati–Pasechnik list 9, whereas Kumar assigns 6 by a misread general bound. The length-19 formula meets Kumar’s numerical value and halves the smallest independent order substantiated in this search. [Craigen (1995), pp. 248–250](https://www.academia.edu/52596913/Signed_groups_sequences_and_the_asymptotic_existence_of_Hadamard_matrices) · [Kharaghani and Tayfeh-Rezaie (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.20043)

The original independent-two-pair family misses exponents 4 through 8 at odd part 2999. Enlarging the Du–Jiang Corollaries 3–5 length search to 95,968 also gives no witness through exponent 8. Corollary 6 at exponent 6 allows a sum of two products of scalar complex Golay lengths, but the length enumeration gives no representation of 2999. Its other case at exponent 6 needs a complex base-sequence length sum 2999, since 2999 is prime. Craigen's Theorem 6(1) with two real pairs needs binary base sequences with half total length 1499. The simple real-Golay sum, consecutive lengths through 43 or attached to a binary Golay length, and Turyn-type seeds through 46 do not provide these. The 1997 Corollary 10 instead needs four equal-length complementary binary sequences of length 2998 = 2·1499. The products in its §4 do not supply this from the tabulated small ingredients. A separate possible route is the 1996 ternary packing problem with weights 2873 and 3125, whose sum is 5998 (and scalings and repeated components). No packing witness was located. This finite comparison leaves open other constructions of order 191936. [Craigen–Kharaghani (1996)](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/13/ocr-ajc-v13-p89.pdf) · [Craigen–Kharaghani (1997), §4](https://www.academia.edu/52596923/Hadamard_Matrices_from_Weighing_Matrices_via_Signed_Groups) · [Du–Jiang, pp. 24–25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381)

## Four prime odd parts above the table limit

The 16p constructions for p=4327, 4759, 5407 and 5639 were independently tested against three older signed-group constructions. Kharaghani's 1992 order-four block lengths 1,3,5 times binary Golay lengths give no representation p=a+b even when the two scaled seed choices are allowed independently. Craigen's 1995 Theorem 6(1) at exponent four needs a binary pair of length (p−1)/2. Those lengths are 2163, 2379, 2703 and 2819. None is a binary Golay number. For the 1996 Theorems 13 and 20 there is no arithmetic solution using weights generated by 2, 5, 13, 17 and 29 and the rank-divisibility conditions on the power-of-two seeds. The 1996 check can run at order 16p alone: its formulas at lower candidate orders carry to this power by increasing either seed order by a Sylvester factor. The script prints possible 1996 solutions and then the checks for the 1992 and 1995 formulas. Run `node family-table-four-primes.mjs`. Finite tests against these constructions do not establish historical priority.
