# Historical comparison for the 38 fill orders

Order **125360 = 16·7835** lies below the earlier explicit order **250720 = 8(3840+27500)** with this odd part. Both lengths satisfy the complex Golay product formula (18) of Du and Jiang. Craigen–Holzmann–Kharaghani Corollary 16 gives the real order. This certifies an earlier exponent of five. The Handbook text layer gives a historical exponent of seven. Neither observation establishes the best exponent immediately before the present construction. [Complex Golay product formula](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381) · [Corollary 16](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(01)00162-5)

Brent–Osborn–Smith generated a subset of earlier orders up to 2³¹ to bound gaps. They publish the generating rules and selected gaps. Neither paper prints the status of the 38 full orders. ## Additional check of Seberry's 1987 orthogonal-design constructions

I tested all 38 orders against the output polynomials of Lemmas 1–3 in *Constructing Hadamard matrices via orthogonal designs*. None of the 38 has a divisor above 1000 of form 2(p+1)(p+2), 4(p+1)(p+2) or 4(3r+1)(4r+3) for positive integral parameters. The search ignored input requirements and exhausts possible parameters at 1000 since larger parameters already exceed the largest candidate. [Seberry, Lemmas 1–3](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/112_1987.pdf)

I also tested Seberry's Lemma 4 at the **largest prime factor v** of every candidate N. Write t=v−4(k−λ) for a cyclic difference set with λ=k(k−1)/(v−1). Complementation permits 1≤k≤(v−1)/2. The lemma requires positive t and divisibility by v(t+1), or 2v(t+1) when v≡1 mod 4. For the lead candidate, v=1567 and the only integral parameter pairs (k,λ) are (1,0), (378,91), (406,105), (783,391). Their t values are 1563,419,363,−1 and none qualifies. Choosing v=5 instead requires an orthogonal design at order 12536 for the direct construction at 125360. Setting its indeterminates to 1 would itself supply H(12536). [Seberry, Lemma 4 and its proof](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/112_1987.pdf)

Across all 38 largest prime factors, only three difference-set parameter triples pass these arithmetic conditions. Each can be ruled out for a cyclic group.

| (v,k,λ) | Fill order(s) | Obstruction |
|---|---:|---|
| (3307,1596,770) | 370384, 899504 | k−λ=826=2·7·59. These primes have multiplicative orders 3306,1653,1102 modulo v. Their generated subgroups intersect in order 551. The second multiplier theorem supplies a multiplier of that order. A fixed translate would require k≡0 or 1 mod 551. |
| (3907,1891,915) | 437584 | k−λ=976=16·61. The orders of 2 and 61 are 3906 and 217 modulo v. Their subgroups intersect in order 217. A fixed translate would require k≡0 or 1 mod 217. |
| (2887,1222,517) | 785264 | k−λ=705=3·5·47. Since 3³⁹≡−1 mod 2887, complex conjugation fixes every prime above 3 in the 2887th cyclotomic field. The character sum X of a cyclic difference set would satisfy X conjugate(X)=705. At any prime over 3 the left side has even valuation and the right side has valuation 1, since 3 is unramified. |

In the first two rows use m=k−λ>λ coprime to v in the second multiplier theorem. Since v is prime, multiplication by a nonidentity multiplier fixes zero and has equally sized orbits elsewhere. A multiplier up to translation fixes a translate because its difference from 1 is invertible modulo v. These checks exclude Lemma 4 on the stated factor for all 38. They leave other transfers and the global priority of all 38 undetermined.
