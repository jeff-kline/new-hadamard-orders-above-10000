# Length supply and an infinite separation of order formulas

Write B for the semigroup generated by 2, 10 and 26. Write C for the classical certified complex Golay lengths from equation (18) of Du and Jiang. The two-factor construction certifies every order 16(m+n) with independently chosen m,n in the union of C, 7B, 9B and 15B. This describes the supply obtained by the cited operations. Completeness among all admissible sequences is open. [Du and Jiang, equation (18)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381) · [Kamali and Kharaghani, pp. 142–144](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/18/ocr-ajc-v18-p139.pdf)

Kamali and Kharaghani's Lemma 3 uses the signed selectors (c+d)/2 and (c−d)/2 of a binary pair. Each product coefficient is a signed copy of an incoming coefficient. The authors explicitly point out after Corollary 5 that a product of two dihedral lengths lacks the general commutation guarantee. Their length-19 example uses i, s and is. No faithful real representation with a common conjugator sending each of those three matrices to its transpose can fix both reflections s and is and negate their product i. The definition of a dihedral pair alone therefore does not license adding 19 to the displayed supply. The concatenation proof also uses the aperiodic identities before reducing modulo the output cycle. A periodic identity modulo z^m−1 does not in general imply that identity modulo z^(m+n)−1. [Kamali and Kharaghani](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/18/ocr-ajc-v18-p139.pdf)

## A theorem about distinct odd parts

For every k≥1 put p_k=7+15·2^k. The p_k are distinct odd integers, and 16p_k is obtained by using the length-7 pair and the binary-scaled length-15 pair. For all but finitely many k, no dyadic multiple of p_k is the sum of two elements of C, or the sum of an element of B and an element of C ∪ 7B ∪ 9B ∪ 15B. Thus these two previously available sum formulas miss infinitely many whole odd parts reached by the independent-input construction.

Indeed, a contrary representation has 7+15·2^k = x+y, where x,y are the two lengths scaled by the same rational power of two with integer exponent. Every summand belongs to the positive rational multiplicative group generated by 2,3,5,7,11,13. Dividing by 7 gives the equation x/7+y/7−15·2^k/7=1. The finite-rank multiplicative group theorem gives only finitely many nondegenerate triples. Each triple fixes k. A degenerate triple must have x or y equal to 15·2^k, leaving the other equal to 7. In the complex-plus-complex case neither summand can contain a factor 7. In the binary-plus-other case the binary summand can have neither an odd factor 7 nor an odd factor 3. Both matchings are impossible. [Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0409604)

The comparison covers explicit length families and all their dyadic rescalings. It leaves other Hadamard constructions, including products, longer sums and prime-power constructions, outside the conclusion. The finiteness argument gives no effective list of exceptional k here. Since the p_k grow geometrically, at least (log X)/(log 2)−O(1) distinct odd parts up to X satisfy the separation.

The same finiteness statement covers multiplication of the old sums by any rational number supported on a fixed finite set of primes. Enlarge the multiplicative group by that set. In a degenerate matching the ratio of the two input lengths, in one orientation, must equal 7/(15·2^k). Two complex lengths have zero 7-adic valuation in their quotient. In a binary-plus-other sum the factor 7 forces the nonbinary input to belong to 7B and occupy the numerator. The resulting quotient of an element of 7B by an element of B has zero 3-adic valuation, while the required ratio has valuation −1. This also excludes all tensor multipliers generated by any fixed finite list of known orders, for the two comparison formulas.

## Using the unrestricted length-19 seed

There is a separate real construction of order **16(m+n)** for an unrestricted dihedral pair of length m and a binary Golay pair of length n. This adds 19B+B at the same order multiplier. Write u for a two-dimensional signed-permutation letter and replace it by the four-dimensional signed permutation diag(u,uᵀ). For Laurent polynomials, transposition of each coefficient with the variable fixed defines an anti-automorphism τ commuting with the polynomial adjoint. On the commutative Laurent algebra generated by the four Hall polynomials of one dihedral pair and their adjoints, τ is multiplicative. Hence P ↦ diag(P,τ(P)) preserves all within-pair identities. Scalar binary letters commute with the new coefficients. Interchanging the two halves conjugates each new letter to its transpose. Using that swap in the common reversal of the signed array and filling with H₄ proves the claim. Lemma 3 supplies every scaled unrestricted pair in 19B. [Kamali and Kharaghani, definition and Lemma 3](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/18/ocr-ajc-v18-p139.pdf)

Two unrestricted dihedral pairs independently give real order **32(m+n)**. On two tensor factors form P⊗I₂ and I₂⊗Q. Then apply P ↦ diag(P,τ(P)) to the resulting jointly commutative Hall algebra with τ now transposing each four-dimensional coefficient. The same swap and reversal work in degree eight, and H₈ fills the array. This avoids separate doubling for each side.

## Historical exponent table above odd part 2,999

The second edition of the Handbook places Table V.1.53 on p. 278 and following. Đoković explicitly describes its scope as odd integers below 10,000 and the least exponent known to the compilers. His corrections concern order 4n. They do not supply the other exponents in the underlying table. The editor's update identifies the first page of the table. I could verify neither the historical exponent for 4,327, 4,759 or 5,407 nor a complete transcription from these sources. [Handbook, Part V](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781420010541.pt5) · [Editor’s corrections](https://site.uvm.edu/jdinitz/?page_id=404) · [Đoković, pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.2043)

Seberry's 1978 paper explains on p. 279 that her computer listing covered odd parts below 10,000 but prints only those below 2,000. Geramita and Seberry subsequently printed the expanded list in Appendix K, pp. 415–425, of their 1979 book. The 1989 construction paper refers to that list and tabulates only its own improvements. The 1992 Seberry–Yamada Table A.17 stops at 2,999. An omitted row from the later correction papers cannot supply a historical exponent above that bound.
