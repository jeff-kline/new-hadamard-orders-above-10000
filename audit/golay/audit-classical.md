## Prime-power and additive constructions

**The exponent 6 baseline for 1499 follows from results available by 2005.** Kharaghani and Tayfeh-Rezaie found a Turyn-type quadruple of lengths 36, 36, 36, 35. The usual concatenation gives base sequences of lengths 36, 36, 71, 71. Craigen and Kharaghani (1997), §4 items 2 and 10, give a normal sequence of length 7 and four complementary binary sequences of length 7(36+71)=749. From the latter four sequences A,B,C,D form the four concatenations (A,B), (A,−B), (C,D), (C,−D). Their summed aperiodic autocorrelations vanish at nonzero shifts by cancellation of the cross terms, so these are four complementary sequences of length 1498. Corollary 10 of the 1997 paper now yields H(64·1499)=H(95,936). The 1997 paper itself explicitly records only H(128·1499). This establishes the exponent in Kumar's table independently of his citation to an asymptotic bound. [Kharaghani–Tayfeh-Rezaie (2005), TT(36)](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcd.20043) · [Craigen–Kharaghani (1997), §4 and Corollary 10](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008241809111) · [Livinskyi, Theorems 4.7, 4.15 and 4.16](https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/bitstream/handle/1993/8915/Livinskyi_Ivan.pdf?sequence=1)

I factored every integer `2^j p − 1` from j = 1 through the target exponent. Every entry in the table below is composite with at least two distinct prime factors. None supplies Paley I or II, including via doubling from a proper divisor. The same test removes Turyn's direct Williamson construction of odd order p, which requires the prime power 2p−1. Yamada's Theorems 4 and 5 require the odd prime power p−2. The common prime-power specialization of Miyamoto's 8p construction requires the prime power 2p−3.

For a Hadamard divisor `d=2^j p`, j≥2, the four classical cyclic Legendre-pair series require a prime length d/2−1, a prime-power d−1, a power-of-two d/2, or d/2−1 equal to a product of twin primes. The factorizations exclude the first two conditions. The odd part excludes the latter two because a product r(r+2) would make d/2 a square. The same odd-part argument excludes regular Hadamards, the twin-prime-power difference-set construction, and their doublings. None of these divisors equals 4(1+q+q²) or 2(q²(q+2)+1) for integer q≥1, by direct substitution up to q=129 and monotonicity thereafter. This checks Spence and Mathon even before the field conditions. ## Recursive inputs

* Complex Miyamoto gives a route to 8p if a quaternary Hadamard matrix of order p−1 exists. Zaitsev's 4 November 2026 public list of unknown quaternary orders explicitly includes 1566 and 1498. His upper limit is 2000. Kumar's public search notes cover 2886 (N=1443) and record no witness, with cases still unresolved. The public source gives a self-contained version of the transfer for primes p≡3 mod 4. [Zaitsev's quaternary census](https://www.cs.uleth.ca/~zaitsevv/quaternary.html) · [Kumar's complex Miyamoto construction](https://github.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/blob/main/hadamard/COMPLEX_MIYAMOTO.md) · [Kumar, search notes §105](https://github.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/blob/main/search/NOTES.md)
* Seberry–Yamada Corollaries 8.14 and 8.18 furnish H(8p) given four Williamson-type matrices of order (p−1)/2. The required orders are 783, 1443 and 749. Their Table A.1 has key wn and exponent 3 at 783 and 1443. Its legend explicitly says this means four Williamson-type matrices of twice the listed odd part, giving orders 1566 and 2886. Row 749 has no recorded construction. A dense Hadamard matrix at four times one of the required orders, or an even-order Williamson quartet, does not by itself supply this hypothesis. [Seberry–Yamada, §8 and Appendix A.2](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/175_1992.pdf)
* For Cooper–Wallis, any relevant order has form 4tw. Since each odd part p is prime, exactly one of t,w must contain it. The candidate lengths for the p-bearing side are 1567, 3134, 6268 for the first order, 2887, 5774, 11548 for the second, and 1499, 2998, 5996, 11992 for the third. None occurs among the T-matrix seeds in the 2026 table, binary Golay lengths g, sums g+h, lengths 2g+1, known small BS(n+1,n) lengths 2n+1 through n=43, or Turyn-type seed lengths 3n−1 from even n≤46. This enumeration used all binary Golay lengths 2^a 10^b 26^c below 12000. Yang multiplication with an odd factor greater than one must use the target prime itself as that factor. The half-lengths (p−1)/2 above are odd, exceed 43, and are outside the binary Golay family. No known normal or near-normal input at one of these lengths was located. This test covers the cited seed lists and formulas, not all possible T-matrices, orthogonal designs or Williamson quartets. [Seberry–Yamada, §5.3–5.5](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/175_1992.pdf) · [Đoković (2010), §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.2043) · [Egan et al. (2026), Tables 3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.27175v2)
* For the Kronecker, Agaian and Craigen–Seberry–Zhang product rules, the unique odd prime must already divide one input. For H(4a), H(4b) → H(8ab) and H(4a), H(4b), H(4c), H(4d) → H(16abcd), its input then has smaller 2-adic exponent. The public catalogs identify no such input. [Seberry–Yamada, Appendix A.1](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/175_1992.pdf) · [Brent–Osborn–Smith, Lemma 15](https://maths-people.anu.edu.au/~brent/pd/rpb253.pdf)

Cati–Pasechnik report coverage through 1,208. I inspected its documentation and used the published Cati–Pasechnik table. The local environment has no Sage executable. None of these databases certifies universal historical priority.
