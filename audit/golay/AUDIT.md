# Independent audit of orders 25,072, 46,192 and 47,968

24 November 2026

The construction gives ordinary real Hadamard matrices at all three orders. Each halves the smallest order we could substantiate previously for the same odd part. Across the sources and theorem substitutions examined, we found no earlier construction of any of the three targets. Historical priority remains qualified.

| Target | Lengths in the verified construction | Smallest earlier order located with this odd part | Earlier derivation |

**25,072 and 46,192.** The four comparison lengths in the first two rows satisfy the published complex Golay product conditions. Corollary 16 of Craigen, Holzmann and Kharaghani gives the orders in the third column. The same test at the targets and their possible Hadamard divisors found no pair of listed lengths that works. The 1998 dihedral-plus-binary construction also misses both. The expanded ternary check found no admissible earlier substitution. Its apparent representation 2,887 = 625 + 3·754 would need three orthogonal sign matrices of order four satisfying the seed identities. Those identities force their common rank to be 4/3. **47,968.** Kharaghani and Tayfeh-Rezaie supply base sequences of lengths 71,71,36,36. A normal sequence system of length 7 gives four complementary binary sequences of length 7(71+36) = 749. Multiplication by the binary Golay length 2 gives four of length 1,498. Corollary 10 of Craigen and Kharaghani now gives 95,936. The coefficient in that corollary is 64. The same paper explicitly lists 191,872 using a different input. Kumar's frontier has the correct comparison order, but transcribes the logarithmic coefficient in Craigen's 1995 Theorem 9 as 1/16. The theorem uses base 64, hence 1/6 with a base-two logarithm. Its general bound at 1,499 gives exponent 10. The four-sequence derivation above supplies exponent 6 independently. The strongest unresolved alternative found at 47,968 comes from Craigen and Kharaghani's 1996 ternary construction. The identity 125 + 2,873 = 2,998 has published complementary weights on both sides. Applying the theorem requires four disjoint component supports that together fill the available positions. Two independent audits examined the published products. Their spans exceed the available lengths, including for the two relevant power-of-two scalings. A different packing remains possible. Brent, Osborn and Smith's 2013 table explicitly places 47,968 in a gap between 47,964 and 47,988 and describes the limits of their catalog in footnote 10. ## What the construction contributes

The generalized Goethals–Seidel array is classical. Kharaghani's 1992 Theorem 9 even has the same order formula 4k(m+n), for suitable block Golay inputs of block size k. His Lemma 7 requires pairwise amicability of the coefficient blocks, including two blocks on the same side. Our length-seven input contains the letters 1 and i. With K the skew-symmetric matrix representing i on the first tensor factor, the filled blocks F = H₄ and G = KH₄ satisfy FGᵀ = −4K and GFᵀ = 4K. Thus the actual inputs violate that hypothesis. The 1998 dihedral theorem uses a binary companion. Placing two independently chosen admissible dihedral pairs on commuting tensor factors provides the input to the classical array. We found no earlier statement of that substitution or published alternate inputs giving these three orders. [Kharaghani 1992, Lemma 7 and Theorem 9](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/6/ocr-ajc-v6-p293.pdf#page=6) · [Kamali and Kharaghani 1998, Theorem 7](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/18/ocr-ajc-v18-p139.pdf) · [Seberry 2017, §4.15, Definition 4.22 and example](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-59032-5_4)

## Verification and scope

An Astra examiner reconstructed the tensor substitution from the claim and the 1998 source before seeing the proof or code. The examiner then multiplied the array, checked the three printed dihedral seeds by separate matrix convolution, and reran both exact Gram checks at all three targets. The two Gram algorithms share the deterministic entry function. Bibliographic checking uncovered a truncated binary word in the 1998 print source. The complete length-26 pair used by the implementation agrees with Jedwab and Parker's equation (11). [Initial derivation](audit-initial.md) · [Proof and implementation audit](audit-proof.md) · [Complete binary seed, p. 2](https://www.sfu.ca/~jed/Papers/Jedwab%20Parker.%20Golay%20Barker.%202009.pdf#page=2)

The priority checks cover the 1992, 2025 and November 2026 order tables, classical direct constructions and doubling, several recursive and signed-group constructions, scalar complex and dihedral Golay series, and ternary complementary products. For Shen et al.'s 2024 Table 2, the iterated Turyn family gives lengths 2ᵏ(L+1)−1 for L in {1,2,3,4,5,6,7,12,14}. These lengths are odd, so a direct four-sequence construction followed by doubling would require the odd prime itself as the sequence length. The odd parts of 1,568, 2,888 and 1,500 are respectively 49, 361 and 375, each too large to divide any listed L+1. The other two families attributed to the paper in that table use the base and Turyn-type lengths already checked. The catalogs are incomplete and the family checks depend on the published inputs available.
