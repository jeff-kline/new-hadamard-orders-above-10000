## Attribution of the array and sequence substitution

Kharaghani (1992), Lemma 7 and Theorem 9, p. 298, already splices two block Golay pairs of lengths m,n using precisely the four positive and negative concatenations and obtains ordinary order 4k(m+n) for block size k. His hypotheses require every dense sign block to be amicable with every other. The degree-four dihedral construction cannot be inserted by simply filling its coefficients with H₄. Within the same printed length-7 pair there are letters 1 and i. With F = H₄ and G = (i⊗I₂)H₄, FGᵀ = −4(i⊗I₂) while GFᵀ = 4(i⊗I₂). The nontrivial lengths 3 and 5 at block size four and their binary Golay multiples also miss all three orders when both summands are granted independently. [Kharaghani, 1992](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/6/ocr-ajc-v6-p293.pdf)

The modified array and its degree-two realization are due to Craigen. The two-factor construction fits the older abstract generalized Goethals–Seidel identity exactly. Seberry's Definition 4.22 and the example immediately following it impose the formal commutation of all xᵢ and xᵢᵀ, the identity r²=1 and the relation r xᵢ r=xᵢᵀ. Substitute the four block circulants and the reversing involution J. The identity is polynomial, so even a singular input is admissible (one can first add a generic scalar multiple of the identity to all four matrices, then specialize to zero). The further ingredient to attribute precisely is the construction of suitable inputs by representing the two dihedral coefficient systems on independent tensor factors. I found no earlier source here stating the implication from two such independent dihedral pairs. Kamali and Kharaghani's Theorem 7 uses a binary second pair. Their Lemma 3 supplies the binary scaling operation. Craigen, Holzmann and Kharaghani's Theorems 10, 15 and Corollary 16 permit two scalar complex Golay pairs and give order 8(m+n). [Craigen, Theorems 9–10](https://combinatorialpress.com/article/jcmcc/Volume%2015/vol-15-paper%2013.pdf) · [Seberry, §4.15, pp. 129–131](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-59032-5_4) · [Kamali and Kharaghani, 1998](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/18/ocr-ajc-v18-p139.pdf) · [Craigen, Holzmann and Kharaghani, 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(01)00162-5)

## The earlier ternary construction

Craigen and Kharaghani's Theorems 13 and 20 use seed matrices and disjoint ternary complementary pairs. The weight of a pair is the total number of nonzero coordinates of its two words. In Theorem 20(9) the h+k pairs must have their 2(h+k) supports mutually disjoint in a common interval of length hw+kv. They therefore partition that interval. The paper's Corollary 23 guarantees a common length hℓ_w+kℓ_v, which can exceed the prescribed length. Theorem 24 gives special exact packings for small weights 5 and 13 and equal powers with enough copies. [Craigen and Kharaghani, pp. 98–103](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/13/ocr-ajc-v13-p89.pdf)

I enumerated M,N in {1,2,4,8} with 4MN dividing the target, h=1,…,M and k=1,…,N, and the two expressions of Theorem 13 and nine of Theorem 20. A seed order divisible by the target's odd prime would itself require an existing Hadamard matrix divisible by that prime, of order at most one quarter of the target. That would settle the row independently by doubling. For the remaining seed orders this enumeration allows more than the paper establishes. I enumerated every weight up to 11992 generated by 2,5,13 and, separately, the enlarged list 2,5,13,17,29 from subsequent ternary work. None of the exact orders has even an arithmetic solution using the first list. With the second list only Theorem 20(9) has arithmetic solutions. [1996, Theorems 13 and 20](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/13/ocr-ajc-v13-p89.pdf) · [Craigen et al., later weights and minimum lengths, §4 and Table 4](https://www.academia.edu/6809675/Further_explorations_into_ternary_complementary_pairs)

The seed requirements exclude two illusory decompositions. The (N,k)-orthogonal set in a seed satisfies CᵢCⱼᵀ=0 for distinct i,j and ΣCᵢCᵢᵀ=NkI. Its row spaces are mutually orthogonal. Their ranks sum to N by the second identity. Choose orthonormal bases Uᵢ of those row spaces and write Cᵢ=TᵢUᵢᵀ. The square matrix T=[T₁ … Tₖ] has TTᵀ=NkI, hence TᵀT=NkI. Taking traces gives N²=Nk rank(Cᵢ), so k divides N. This excludes 2887=625+3·754 with the required (4,3) seed. For (2,2) any Cᵢ has rank one and equals uvᵀ for sign vectors u,v. Amicability with H₂ forces H₂v proportional to u. But H₂v has one zero and one entry of magnitude two. This also excludes (2,2) seeds. [Seed definitions, pp. 92 and 98](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/13/ocr-ajc-v13-p89.pdf)

At 47968 the exact remaining obligation from this enumeration is **two disjoint ternary complementary pairs of weights 125c and 2873c whose four supports partition an interval of length 2998c**, for c=1,2 or 4. These cases would yield ordinary orders 11992c, each sufficient for 47968 by doubling. Here 125=5³ and 2873=13²·17. The standard disjoint products in Theorem 21 cannot directly supply the large input at those lengths using factors from the cited primitive weights through 29. Each normalized product has span equal to the product of its factor spans because at each occupied outer position the two output sequences use the entire inner support union or its reversal. A disjoint weight-13 pair needs span at least 14 and weight 17 needs at least 18, since filling the entire odd interval would produce a binary Golay pair of odd length larger than one via U±V. Grant the full weight-26 pair when pairing 2 with 13. The minimum span bounds for 2873c under these products are 3528, 6552 and 12168, exceeding 2998, 5996 and 11992 respectively. More general shifts of individual words, other ternary constructions or periodic methods could yield different supports. No such packing was located here. [1996, Theorems 20–24](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/13/ocr-ajc-v13-p89.pdf) · [Later primitive TCPs](https://www.academia.edu/81900604/An_update_on_primitive_ternary_complementary_pairs)

## Other principal sources

- Craigen 1995, Theorem 5, supplies conversion from a signed group Hadamard to an ordinary one given an appropriate monomial representation and a dense Hadamard matrix. Its Theorem 6 gives different additive forms in binary complementary inputs with certain scalar complex lengths allowed. At exponent four its binary single-pair case needs real Golay length 783 or 1443. At exponent five and odd part 1499, the shifts by odd complex lengths 3,5,11,13 require real lengths 748,747,744,743 respectively. For the additional form involving a complex length, the real and complex lengths would have to sum to 749. No known binary/complex input pair supplies this. [Craigen, 1995, pp. 247–249](https://www.academia.edu/52596913/Signed_groups_sequences_and_the_asymptotic_existence_of_Hadamard_matrices)
- The 1997 signed-group paper explicitly obtains 2⁷·1499 via Corollary 10. Its Theorem 12 combines two disjoint signed-group matrices under commutation and quasisymmetry or amicability hypotheses. The paper's final two-length block example uses a commuting symmetric second input supplied by binary Golay pairs and a larger power of two. [Craigen and Kharaghani, pp. 55–58](https://www.academia.edu/52596923/Hadamard_Matrices_from_Weighing_Matrices_via_Signed_Groups)
- Egan's §5, Theorem 5.1, constructs a two-by-two block matrix from one phased complementary pair, invoking commutation of the circulants in its proof. Ghaderpour's Theorems 5 and 7 concern disjoint quasisymmetric ingredients and encode scalar complex Golay lengths, respectively. Ghaderpour et al.'s Theorem 3.5 gives a complex design whose minimum power-of-two factor is 2⁶ before conversion to real. Du–Jiang §III and Corollaries 5–6 concern scalar four-phase sequences and specified signed-group combinations. I found no stated two-independent-dihedral-pair implication in these sources. [Egan, §5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2019.111795) · [Ghaderpour](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.07668) · [Ghaderpour et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.02806) · [Du and Jiang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381)
