# Mixed Golay orders beyond odd part 2999

For numerical order at most 200,000 and odd part greater than 2,999, the mixed and two-dihedral rules together give **1,502 distinct orders**. Of these, 1,466 follow directly or by doubling from the two-complex-length rule, the dihedral-plus-binary rule, Du–Jiang’s dense-quad arithmetic or Paley I/II. The remaining 36 require other comparisons. This enumeration includes composite odd parts and every attainable exponent. The companion `family-beyond-history.mjs` prints all 36. The dense-quad test uses the explicit arithmetic in Du–Jiang §III, including expansion of the base-sequence range to 43. Positive lengths make the bounded operations exact for the tested sets. [Du–Jiang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381) · [Kamali–Kharaghani](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/18/ocr-ajc-v18-p139.pdf)

The script also examines all primes p congruent to 7 modulo 8 with 2,999 < p ≤ 100,000. There are 149 distinct p for which 16p has a mixed or two-dihedral witness. For 41 of them, the previous sequence rules miss both p and 2p as input to the order-eight transfer, and each of 2p−1, 4p−1, 8p−1, 16p−1, p−2, 2p−3 has more than one prime factor. The first five are 4327, 4759, 5407, 5639 and 6343. This last filter conservatively drops primes with p−2 a prime power even when the additional conference or skew hypothesis for the corresponding construction is unknown. Neither 36 nor 41 counts previously unknown Hadamard orders.

## Checks at 69,232 and 76,144

| p | Mixed witness for 16p | 2p−1 | 4p−1 | 8p−1 | 16p−1 |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4327 | 7 + 4320 | 17·509 | 3³·641 | 5·7·23·43 | 3·47·491 |
| 4759 | 7 + 4752 | 31·307 | 3⁴·5·47 | 11·3461 | 3·17·1493 |

The complex lengths factor as 4320 = 2⁵·3³·5 and 4752 = 2⁴·3³·11. The displayed factorizations remove Paley I/II at each allowed divisor having odd part p. The independent prime-power screens give p−2 = 5²·173 and 67·71, and 2p−3 = 41·211 and 5·11·173, respectively. Both p are 3 modulo 4, so ordinary Miyamoto I lacks the real input H(p−1). In a Kronecker, Agaian or Craigen–Seberry–Zhang product, an input would already have to contain the unique odd prime p. Twin-prime-power, regular and doubled square constructions cannot have a squarefree prime odd part. The historical Paley I construction gives order 32·4759 because 152287 is prime. [Brent–Osborn–Smith, proof of Lemma 15](https://maths-people.anu.edu.au/~brent/pd/rpb253.pdf)

Two structured alternatives are especially relevant. Seberry–Yamada’s Corollary 8.14 would produce 8p if four Williamson-type matrices exist at order 2163 = 3·7·103 or 2379 = 3·13·61, respectively. These exceed the printed end of Table A.1 at 1999. The classical Turyn construction for these two Williamson orders would need p−2 prime power. The authors’ key wj instead requires a symmetric conference matrix of order 1082 or 1190, using primes 2161 and 2377. Order 1082 fails the two-squares condition since 1081 = 23·47. At 1190, the number 1189 = 29·41 satisfies that necessary condition, but fails the Paley and Mathon parameter tests. No corresponding symmetric conference certificate was located. [Seberry–Yamada, §8 and Appendix A](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/175_1992.pdf) · [Đoković, Theorem 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.2043)

Kumar’s complex Miyamoto construction would also produce 8p, from a quaternary Hadamard matrix of order 4326 or 4758. His quaternary Yamada–Kiyasu construction can supply these inputs with the same primes 2161, 2377 from a quaternary conference matrix of order 1082 or 1190. The real two-squares obstruction does not exclude a quaternary certificate. Kumar’s checked-in complex certificates lack both Hadamard inputs. His notes list even the quaternary Hadamard order 1190 as open in the cited search. A quaternary conference matrix and a quaternary Hadamard matrix at that order have distinct hypotheses. [Complex Miyamoto](https://github.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/blob/main/hadamard/COMPLEX_MIYAMOTO.md) · [Quaternary conference arrays](https://github.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/blob/main/complex-hadamard/CONFERENCE.md)

Neither p occurs in Đoković’s two lists of 4p corrections (142 and 42 corrections, respectively). The papers concern the smaller orders 17308 and 19036. The candidates also have divisors 34616 and 38072 whose existence would supersede the new exponent. Seberry’s 1978 paper says its computerized listing covered odd parts below 10,000 and prints only those below 2,000. Geramita–Seberry (1979), pp. 415–425, is cited as the source for the older exponents through 10,000 by an ensuing paper whose published corrections omit 4327 and 4759. Seberry–Yamada’s 1992 appendix explicitly describes a computer table of odd parts below 40,000. Its printed ordinary table stops at 3,000. Brent–Osborn–Smith report a computer-generated subset up to 2³¹. Their article gives the rules and selected gaps. Smith’s public 2020 matrix generator is a different program. These sources show why the absence of an indexed order or a recipe in this scan cannot establish priority. [Đoković (2009)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.5091) · [Đoković (2010)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.2043) · [Seberry (1978), p. 279](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/067_1978.pdf) · [Earlier-exponent comparison to the 1979 book, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700030871) · [Seberry–Yamada, p. 535](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/175_1992.pdf) · [Smith’s public generator](https://oeis.org/A007299/a007299.c.txt)

I did not independently retrieve the numerical entries in the 2006 Handbook V.1, Table 1.53. Its exponent table covers odd parts below 10,000. Its correction lists alone cannot establish the status of an unlisted correction. No global priority inference follows from the two numerical witnesses. [Handbook editors’ site](https://site.uvm.edu/jdinitz/?page_id=312)

Among the 41 prime cases, exactly one requires two dihedral inputs at exponent four in the searched supply. The order is **151664 = 16·9479**, with lengths 9464 = 7·2·26² and 15. No decomposition of 9479 as one of the three binary-scaled dihedral lengths plus a standard complex Golay length exists. The final line of the companion script reproduces this comparison. This gives a concrete example where allowing two dihedral factors extends the length support beyond the one-dihedral rule.
