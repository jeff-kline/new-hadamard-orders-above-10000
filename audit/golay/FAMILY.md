# How many further ordinary Hadamard orders?

24 November 2026

The independent-pair construction lowers three entries in Kumar's frontier, at orders **25,072, 46,192 and 47,968**. Testing every odd part through 2,999 yields zero additional improvements. The initial comparison identified 94 additional candidate orders through 1,000,000, with 70 distinct odd parts. Follow-up work recovered older constructions for **nine**. Historical priority remains unresolved for **85 orders at 65 odd parts**. The numerical counts below document the initial comparison. ## Full length family and unbounded conclusion

Let B be the multiplicative semigroup generated by 2, 10 and 26, including 1. Let C consist of lengths 2ᵃ3ᵇ5ᶜ11ᵈ13ᵉ with nonnegative exponents satisfying b+c+d+e ≤ a+min(a,c+e)+1. Both inputs range independently over S = C ∪ 7B ∪ 9B ∪ 15B. The transfer proves H(16(m+n)) for every pair from S. The census includes every such substitution under the cutoff, including composite odd parts. Additional admissible sequences could enlarge S. The audit established the general matrix identity and checked the three original matrices entrywise. The additional ledger rows use the general identity with certified lengths. [Construction proof](mixed-proof.md) · [Kamali and Kharaghani](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/18/ocr-ajc-v18-p139.pdf) · [Du and Jiang, equation (18)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381)

Exactly one input must lie in 7B for a possible improvement over older constructions. If neither input lies there, 2m and 2n are both in C, so the old scalar formula 8(2m+2n) gives the target. This uses 18,30 ∈ C and closure under binary Golay scaling. If both inputs are 7g and 7h, the two binary Golay pairs give base sequences and hence T-sequences of length g+h. Cooper–Wallis with Williamson matrices of order seven gives H(28(g+h)), which reaches the target after two doublings. These arguments apply at every size. [Scalar construction](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(01)00162-5) · [Classical constructions and inputs](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/175_1992.pdf)

There is also a positive unbounded result. For all but finitely many k ≥ 1, the odd part 7 + 15·2ᵏ escapes every dyadic rescaling of the older scalar-plus-scalar and binary-plus-dihedral length formulas, while the new construction gives 16(7 + 15·2ᵏ). The proof uses the finite-rank unit equation. Other Hadamard construction families can still reach these orders. Global historical yield across the infinite family is unknown. [Proof and scope](family-supply-audit.md) · [Evertse, Schlickewei and Schmidt, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0409604)

## Historical coverage and reproducibility

Three independent Astra investigations examined the length hypotheses, every frontier row, and older constructions above the frontier. The broader census includes Paley, Yamada and Miyamoto variants, conference and Williamson-type inputs, Cooper–Wallis, real complementary sequences, scalar and dihedral Golay formulas, Du–Jiang including Corollary 6, checked-in Kumar orders, doubling, Agaian, and Craigen–Seberry–Zhang products. Against Cati–Pasechnik alone the formula lowers 34 rows. Against Kumar it lowers three. Du–Jiang already gives 47,920. [Cati–Pasechnik, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18897v2) · [Kumar's pinned repository](https://github.com/vatskishan03/Hadamard/tree/d885022144ba6bf4efa8315e14871a3736947527) · [Du–Jiang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.15381)

The 1979 Geramita–Seberry appendix and the 2006 Handbook extend to odd parts below 10,000. We could verify their range and published corrections, but could not recover the underlying numerical rows for the four smallest candidates. Seberry–Yamada also describe a computer table extending to odd parts below 40,000. Brent, Osborn and Smith generated a subset of known orders through 2³¹ and published selected gaps. Their list of gaps ends before the first additional candidate here. Other published inputs, including ternary packings, could lower the candidate count. [Handbook, Part V](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781420010541.pt5) · [Đoković's table corrections](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.2043) · [Seberry and Yamada](https://documents.uow.edu.au/~jennie/WEBPDF/175_1992.pdf) · [Brent, Osborn and Smith, Lemma 15 and Table 1](https://maths-people.anu.edu.au/~brent/pd/rpb253.pdf)
