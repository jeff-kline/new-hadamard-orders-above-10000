**Array compatibility.** In the dihedral algebra take i²=−1, s²=1, is=−si with adjoint given by transpose in its standard signed-permutation representation. For u in {±1,±i,±s}, s u s=u*. The same holds in the complex alphabet. Embed each dihedral letter as u⊗I₂ and each complex letter as I₂⊗v. The two embedded Laurent algebras commute with one another. Within the dihedral pair the two Hall polynomials and their two adjoints commute by the definition of a dihedral Golay pair. In the complex pair this is automatic. For input pairs (a,b) and (c,d) of lengths m,n, form block circulants from the four concatenations (a,c), (a,−c), (b,d), (b,−d). Their products with their adjoints sum to 4(m+n)I by adding the positive and negative concatenations and using the two aperiodic identities. Pairwise commutation with the adjoints survives substitution of the cyclic shift. With R the position reversal, J=R⊗s⊗s satisfies J²=I and JZJ=Zᵀ for each of the four block circulants, by the coefficient identity above. These are precisely the hypotheses used in the modified Goethals–Seidel array. Each nonzero scalar-position block in the resulting array is a signed permutation of degree 4. Right-multiplying every such block by the same real H₄ fills it with signs, and multiplies the entire Gram by 4. This verifies the asserted order 16(m+n) independently. The older Theorem 7 gives 8(m+n) when the second pair is binary. **Reproduction.** Run `node mixed-priority.mjs` from this directory. All sets are truncated at 12000, exceeding 4·2999. Inputs and outputs are lengths, with no sequence search. The columns report the exponents k≤5 for which the respective construction reaches 2^k p directly. The first three tests use the mixed rule, the 1998 dihedral rule with the binary pair, and the sum of two complex Golay lengths. The last is the Du–Jiang §III denser-quad recursion and Corollaries 4 and 5. It includes the widened small base-sequence range through s=43, compared with s=38 in their formula (19), and it includes S₁B. Products or sums of positive lengths cannot bring an intermediate greater than the cutoff back below it. The zero element in the published sets gives only zero when multiplied. Du–Jiang Corollary 6 yields no extra case here. For outer 2-adic valuation at most five its only nonempty ingredient form is a single product of two complex Golay lengths, with prefactor 16, and no prime odd part on the three rows divides such a product. Livinskyi’s Theorems 3.10–3.11 with a single binary or complex Golay pair require (p−1)/2 of the relevant type; these lengths are 783, 1443, and 749, all containing other prime factors. The binary two-pair form already costs an outer factor 64.
